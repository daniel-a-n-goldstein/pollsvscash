The Hope Index

The audacity to hope the polls are wrong. People are putting real money on races that are polled almost daily, and sometimes the money and the polls disagree. Polls are cheap talk; bets cost something. Is the money real sentiment, or partisan cheerleading with a wallet? The Hope Index tracks the spread, race by race, until November. Then we'll see where the smart money was.

Amber: the money is more hopeful than the polls. Teal: more doubtful.

How the hope wedge is computed

Market prices are Polymarket prices, fetched daily. The poll side is the RealClearPolling average for each race, refreshed weekly and converted to a win probability with a simple polling-error model (σ = 5.5 points). Senate control uses Decision Desk HQ's forecast instead, since there is no margin to convert. Wedge = market − polls, in percentage points. A positive wedge may be information the polls haven't caught up to, or it may be bettors' hope. The wedge can't differentiate the two. Wedges near 0% or 100% are inflated by the favorite–longshot bias, since markets rarely price above ~90¢, so those should be read loosely. Market history before launch (Oct 9) is backfilled from Polymarket from mid-August. The poll line before launch is a 28-day rolling average of the individual RCP-listed polls (using at least three polls and not RCP's own average), drawn dashed. Full details: methods & assumptions.
