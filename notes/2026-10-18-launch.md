# Launch post — October 18, 2026

*Posted on LinkedIn. Numbers are as of that morning's board.*

A small side project for the U.S. midterms: The Hope Index.

Polls and prediction markets are both trying to tell us who will win. But polls are cheap talk voting intentions, and bets are not: they're real money. So when they disagree, does the money see something the surveys miss, or is it partisan cheerleading with a wallet?

I built a tracker to measure this "hope" gap for nine Senate races plus control of both chambers. It pulls Polymarket and Kalshi prices daily and RealClearPolling averages weekly, converts both to win probabilities, and shows the difference.

Right now the biggest gap is Iowa: the polls have Turek (D) about tied with Hinson (R), but the money gives Turek only about a 42% chance, 12 points below what the polls imply. On Senate control, the money favors the Democrats more than the Decision Desk HQ forecast does. And the gaps move: Alaska was at 15 points last week and is at 6 now. The polls caught up while the bettors stayed fixed.

After the election, the site scores each race on which was closer, the polls or the money, and I'll write up what happened.

Methods and caveats are on the site. Code and data are on GitHub. Suggestions welcome!
